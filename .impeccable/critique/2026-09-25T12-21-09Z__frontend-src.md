## Design Specificity Verdict

### LLM assessment

**Authored for gendertagger where the scholarly work happens; only partially authored in the surrounding shell.**

The review workbench is product-specific: contested and thin-evidence queues, TEI identifiers, authority context, nine independent method outcomes, explicit abstention/error states, canonical labels, and append-only human decisions meet on one surface. An unrelated CRUD or analytics product could not reuse that composition unchanged.

Warm parchment, flat paper panels, hairlines, Annotation Ink selection, Registry Teal actions, monospace vocabulary, and textual status alongside color form a coherent “Annotated Archive” language. The project index, home, and administration surfaces are more interchangeable: centered forms, white cards, and conventional tables remain competent but express little of that archival character.

## Overall Impression

The core scholarly review model is unusually honest and well structured. It preserves uncertainty rather than turning every method output into false confidence. The single biggest opportunity is to make that strong evidence model usable at narrow widths and cognitively manageable before reviewers become experts. Right now, the best part of the product is also its most exhausting surface.

## Emotional Journey

1. **Arrival — credible and calm.** The public explanation is honest about missing source gender and independent methods. Unconfigured publication details are stated rather than invented.
2. **Orientation — competent, then visibly cracked.** Project switching, active navigation, and back links establish location. The persistent red preference failure and literal `nav.collapseSidebar` key immediately lower confidence.
3. **Triage — purposeful, then overloaded.** Contested/thin/all queues and open counts create momentum. Fifteen filters shift the feeling from editorial focus to procedural vigilance.
4. **Decision — evidence-led, but under-reassuring on mobile.** At 390px, the method table expands the page to 470px and clips the “Own confidence” column precisely where trust matters most.
5. **Peak — efficient expert action.** Pressing `2` selected `female`, enabled Save, scrolled to it, and established a strong visible focus state.
6. **End — likely safe, but weakly continued.** Source confirms a focused success message and append-only history after save. The interface lacks an equally visible “saved—continue to next case” rhythm.

## What’s Working

1. **The evidence model stays honest.** Unknown, abstention, technical error, no machine label, method confidence, authority disagreement, source record, and human revision remain distinct.
2. **Safeguards match the scholarly stakes.** Save requires a real choice; nothing is silently autosaved; permanence is stated; revision conflicts preserve newer work; destructive branding reset requires confirmation.
3. **Accessibility foundations are real.** The mobile drawer closes with Esc and restores focus, focus rings are visible, controls have descriptive names, live states use status/alert roles, and meaning is not conveyed by color alone.

## Priority Issues

### 1. **[P1] The mobile review path hides evidence and destroys task continuity**

- **Why it matters:** At 390×844, the page has `scrollWidth=470`; the rightmost “Own confidence” evidence column is outside the viewport. The full filter panel precedes queue, case, evidence, and decision, producing a roughly 3,635px page. A reviewer can commit without seeing all evidence simultaneously.
- **Fix:** At the narrow breakpoint, order the task as **queue → selected case/evidence/decision → collapsed filters**. Replace the wide method table with stacked definition rows. Keep selected person and decision state in a compact sticky case header. Put advanced filters in a labeled disclosure or drawer with an active-filter count.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable adapt`

### 2. **[P1] A nonessential preference failure degrades every signed-in screen**

- **Why it matters:** Language and Theme remain disabled; a red error persists across project, run, review, and admin routes; retry returns to the same state; `nav.collapseSidebar` leaks into the interface. The error text itself measures approximately 4.254:1, below WCAG AA for normal text.
- **Fix:** Repair the `/api/v1/me/preferences` response/seed contract and missing translation. Preserve usable language/theme fallbacks when preferences fail. Retry needs an announced busy state followed by either recovery or a specific next step. Adjust the critical text/surface pairing to at least 4.5:1.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

### 3. **[P2] The signature workbench exposes expert controls before the primary act**

- **Why it matters:** Fifteen filters and nine method rows compete with the actual task: choose one person, understand the disagreement, and decide. Experts gain power, but new and occasional reviewers must first parse the full query model.
- **Fix:** Keep search, order, and review state visible. Move agreement, authority conflict, technical error, and per-method criteria under **More filters**, with active count, removable chips, and **Reset filters**. Add a compact conflict synopsis above the full method evidence.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable distill`

### 4. **[P2] Branding administration separates understanding from editing**

- **Why it matters:** At 390px, the page is approximately 3,645px tall. Users first inspect effective/fallback/status values in a table, then meet the same six concepts again as 18 radios and six controls.
- **Fix:** Make each branding field one unit showing effective value, fallback, override status, and an **Edit override** disclosure. Open only fields being changed. Keep Save/Discard in a sticky action bar with a changed-field summary.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable layout`

### 5. **[P2] Visible product naming violates the lowercase brand rule**

- **Why it matters:** The brand link, home heading, accessible name, browser title, and injected-tab title expose **“Gendertagger”**, while the confirmed product name is always **gendertagger**. This weakens identity and creates avoidable copy drift.
- **Fix:** Centralize the canonical product-name string and use `gendertagger` in visible copy, accessible labels, localized resources, and document metadata.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify`

## Persona Red Flags

### Alex — impatient power reviewer

- **Path:** Projects → completed run → aggregation → Review → contested person → decision.
- **Works:** Numeric decision shortcuts and bulk selection support speed.
- **Red flags:** Alex must scan three queues, 15 filters, and nine evidence rows. There is no visible next/previous-case accelerator. The persistent preference failure adds noise to every route.

### Sam — keyboard/screen-reader-dependent reviewer

- **Path:** Open mobile navigation → Review → queue/person → evidence → decision.
- **Works:** Esc closes the drawer and restores focus; focus rings are strong; radios, statuses, and vocabulary have textual names.
- **Red flags:** The 470px-wide document clips “Own confidence” at a 390px viewport. Selected case and decision are separated by the full filter/list stack. Preference recovery is generic, disabled, and low contrast.

### Casey — distracted mobile reviewer

- **Path:** Open review deep link at 390×844 → adjust queue/filter → choose person → compare evidence → save.
- **Red flags:** The page is roughly 3,635px tall, filters precede the primary task, the evidence table clips horizontally, and Save sits near the end. Touch users receive none of the numeric-shortcut acceleration.

## Minor Observations

- The honest “publication details not configured” state is excellent.
- Project creation appears before existing project cards, prioritizing occasional setup over reopening a project.
- Compact 0.75–0.8125rem typography becomes taxing across long evidence and administration pages.
- The administration table scrolls internally on mobile but gives no visible cue that more columns exist.
- The detector’s 4px authority side-border is real. Replace it with a labeled full-outline/tint treatment if that block is revised.
- Sidebar width animation triggered the layout-animation rule on every route; it is one shared implementation, not four defects.
- Thirteen vocabulary-badge padding warnings and all three alleged text occlusions were detector false positives, not reasons to enlarge the compact vocabulary system.

## Questions to Consider

1. If the signature act is deciding one person from evidence, why must a mobile reviewer traverse the complete filter model first?
2. Should each case behave like one annotated archival folio—source, conflict synopsis, methods, decision, history—rather than the last panel in a long stack?
3. Which filters do editors use every hour, and which are visible mainly because the API can express them?
4. What should reviewers see first when methods disagree: nine raw rows, or a compact conflict synopsis with raw evidence available on demand?
